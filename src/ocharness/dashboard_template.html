The slider and the token scenario (in/out) recompute in live JS the new-plan costs, the critical threshold and the verdict margins from the embedded per-cell tokens, rates and anchor — nothing persisted changes (raw immutable; derivatives regenerate only with versioned parameters). Models with a conclusive measured hit-rate keep it (marked «measured S») and the slider cannot move them; models without a published discount are unmoved: S(x) ≡ S0 for them. A custom value that overrides measurements belongs to a stamped re-run (--s), never to this dashboard.

Verdicts compare what the same monthly budget buys on each system: the new plan sells credits at a per-tier multiplier over money paid ( at this doc's tier: Max $100 → $300 credits), so a new-side figure of $X in credits really costs $X ÷  paid. The margin is stated in paid dollars — example: a task that burns $.00 of new-plan credits costs $1.00 paid. The two price columns are nominal dollars for the token scenario set in the «tokens in» / «tokens out» inputs (default 1M in / 250k out). The new column prices the scenario exactly on the official table (input, cached input and output at their own rates) and follows the S(x) slider. The legacy meter emits a single pp read per bracket with no in/out decomposition (the GPU-time rate was never published), so the legacy column applies the cell's measured pp/1M to the scenario's in+out total — hover for the underlying meter read and the per-task prices; a sub-tick weekly read falls back to the session-derived equivalent, marked «session». The verdict chips are the measured per-task comparison of each cell and do not follow the scenario inputs. The meter-native view lives in the threshold chart below. Every verdict shows its margin (the chip's +X %): how much more expensive the loser is, (loser − winner) ÷ winner — it can exceed 100 % when the loser costs more than twice the winner. A verdict exists only when the margin clears the tie band (>2 meter ticks or >5 % of the cheaper cost); inside it the cell is a tie.
